# Tripura Rahasya Parts 2 and 3: cover guide for Notion Press Cover Creator

## What I could confirm about Notion's rules
- **Cover Creator:** you can upload your own image, which must be **300 DPI or higher**.
- **Barcode:** the ISBN and price barcode is added on the back automatically.
- **Spine:** the title and author are added to the spine automatically.
- **Back cover:** can hold a blurb and an author bio.
- **Image adjustments available:** filters, brightness, transparency and contrast.
- **Not stated by Notion:** bleed, safe zone, file format, colour mode and spine width. Treat the preview in the tool, or the template it gives you, as the final word on those.
- **Spine width:** it depends on page count. Part 1 was 22 mm for 481 pages, which works out to about 0.046 mm per page. On the same paper that gives about **14 mm for Part 2** (about 295–310 pages) and **15–16 mm for Part 3** (about 325–345 pages). Set up the cover only after the final interior PDF is uploaded.

## Keeping the three books a series
| | Part 1 (published) | Part 2 | Part 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Art | Dattatreya, Vrindavan Das (licence to confirm) | **Dattatreya, Raja Ravi Varma** (c. 1910, public domain) | **Ambika (Lalita / Tripura Sundari), Ravi Varma Press** (public domain) |
| Front colour | Inverted, cool blue | Natural warm colours (saffron, gold) | Natural colours, deep green and gold |
| Spine colour | Slate grey-blue | Deep maroon `#7A1F2B` | Deep green `#1E4D3B` |
| Part label | "(PART 1)" in small type | **"PART 2"**, at least twice Part 1's size | **"PART 3"**, at least twice Part 1's size |
| Back quote | Ch 7 v91 | Ch 13 v91 | Ch 22 v105 |

Keep everything else exactly as on Part 1: title font, size and position, subtitle text and position, red author name, back-cover layout, and matte lamination. If a background is too dark for black title text, switch the title to ivory `#F5EDE0` rather than changing the font.

## Images (download them yourself from Wikimedia Commons)
- **Part 2:** search Commons for "Raja Ravi Varma - Dattatreya.jpg", 2,304 × 3,072 px.
  - Licence: public domain. The artist died in 1906, and the print was published before 1931.
  - Resolution: at a 9.25" cover height (with bleed) that's about 330 DPI, which is fine.
  - Prep: crop out any printed caption or press-name strip at the bottom.
- **Part 3:** search Commons for "Ambika from Ravi Varma press.jpg", 1,544 × 2,034 px.
  - Subject: Google Arts & Culture's record of the Ravi Varma Press "Ambika" print describes it as Raja-Rajeshwari / Lalita / Tripura Sundari in the form of Ambika. Check that the Commons file is the same composition.
  - Resolution: about 220 DPI at full height, **below Notion's 300 DPI**. Upscale it 1.5× (for example with Upscayl, or Photoshop's "Preserve Details 2.0") to at least 2,316 × 3,051 px before uploading. Crop out any caption strip.
  - Licence: the painting is public domain (Ravi Varma died in 1906). Commons dates the print only to "early 20th century", so its US status is less certain than Part 2's image.
  - Fallback: Wellcome Collection's "Dattātreya with his four dogs and cow. Chromolithograph by R. Varma." It carries a Public Domain Mark and has a full-size download.
- **Back covers:** use the same image as the front, darkened (brightness about −50%) as on Part 1, or a solid colour matching the spine.

## Steps in Notion Cover Creator (repeat for each part)
1. **Layout:** pick the layout with an image on both front and back. This matches Part 1.
2. **Front background:** upload the prepared image.
   - Part 2: no inversion. Add +10 contrast, plus a warm filter if one is offered.
   - Part 3: no inversion. Add +10 contrast.
3. **Back background:** upload the same image and turn brightness down until the white blurb text is easy to read (Part 1's back is about 50% darker).
4. **Title:** "TRIPURA RAHASYA", in the same font, size and position as Part 1.
5. **Subtitle:** on separate lines:
   - "PART 2" (or "PART 3"), large
   - "SANSKRIT TO ENGLISH TRANSLATION, TRANSLITERATION AND WORD-TO-WORD", same size as Part 1
6. **Author:** "SOHAM HAMSO", in the same red serif as Part 1.
7. **Spine:** the tool adds the title and author. If it lets you set the spine colour, use the colour from the table. If you can edit the spine text, add "II" or "III" at the top.
8. **Blurb:** paste the text below. Keep it out of the bottom-right corner, where the barcode goes.
9. **Preview and Finish:** check the whole wrap, then confirm.

## Back-cover blurbs (Part 1's opening kept; second paragraph is new)

**Part 2**

"Tripura Rahasya" is a revered ancient Sanskrit text that delves into spiritual teachings and philosophy, presented as a dialogue between Lord Dattatreya and his disciple Parasurama. This second volume (Chapters 12–17) carries the teaching forward through two great narratives: a sage's son leads King Mahasena into a world hidden within a mountain, showing that worlds, time and space are images appearing in consciousness, and King Janaka, in his assembly, instructs the sage Ashtavakra on the nature of the Self, on why deep sleep is not realization, until Ashtavakra becomes liberated while living. Every verse is given in Devanagari, transliteration, word-to-word meaning and English translation.

*Chapter 13, Verse 91*
*"Know the waking state as a picture in a mirror, and yourself as consciousness in essence. Become supremely blissful in heart quickly, O king."*

**Part 3**

"Tripura Rahasya" is a revered ancient Sanskrit text that delves into spiritual teachings and philosophy, presented as a dialogue between Lord Dattatreya and his disciple Parasurama. This concluding volume (Chapters 18–22) gathers the essence of the teaching: consciousness without objects, the distinctions among the states of the wise, the Goddess's own Song of Knowledge (Vidya Gita), the tale of the Brahmarakshasa, and the closing vision of the world as the play of Tripura, the one non-dual consciousness. Every verse is given in Devanagari, transliteration, word-to-word meaning and English translation.

*Chapter 22, Verse 105*
*"The unbroken non-dual consciousness power, Tripura, indeed, shining, she indeed is ignorance and knowledge, bondage and liberation, and the means."*

(Both quotes are word for word from the translations in your manuscript.)
