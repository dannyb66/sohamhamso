## Book description (Amazon / Notion)

First line in bold:

**The original Sanskrit of the Tripura Rahasya, verse by verse, with transliteration, word-to-word meaning and English translation. Part 2: Chapters 12 to 17.**

"Tripura Rahasya" (also known as the Haritayana Samhita) is a revered ancient Sanskrit text, a dialogue between Lord Dattatreya and his disciple Parasurama. It blends Advaita Vedanta with teachings on yoga, meditation and the nature of reality, exploring self-realization, the illusion of the material world and the path to liberation. Ramana Maharshi highly esteemed Tripura Rahasya, considering it one of the greatest works that expound Advaita philosophy.

Part 2 continues Dattatreya's teaching and includes the three chapters on the sage Ashtavakra.

Every verse gives you:

• The Sanskrit verse in Devanagari
• Roman transliteration
• Word-to-word meaning
• English translation

For seekers of self-inquiry and non-duality, and for students of Sanskrit, Vedanta and Shakta philosophy who want to read the text in the original.

"Know the waking state as a picture in a mirror, and yourself as consciousness in essence." (Chapter 13, Verse 91)

The series: Part 1 (Chapters 1 to 11), Part 2 (Chapters 12 to 17) and Part 3 (Chapters 18 to 22).

## Author bio

Soham Hamso takes its name from so'haṃ haṃsaḥ, the ajapa-japa breath mantra: haṃ on the out-breath, sa on the in-breath. Read forward or backward it mirrors itself, an image of the reflection (pratibimba) at the heart of the non-dual teachings. Under this name the author publishes classical Sanskrit texts in a form that keeps the original in view: the Devanagari verse, its transliteration, a word-by-word meaning and an English translation, so readers can study the source itself and not only a paraphrase. The Tripura Rahasya series presents the 22 chapters of the text in three parts. Soham Hamso also runs Sohamhamso.org, a free, non-profit and donation-funded online reader for the Sanskrit texts of Kashmir Shaivism, Trika and the Shakta tradition, among them the Shiva Sutras, the Vijnana Bhairava Tantra, the Spanda Karikas and Abhinavagupta's Tantrasara. It presents each verse in Sanskrit with transliteration, word-by-word glosses and translations in English, with AI-assisted translations into eleven Indian languages, and shares its corpus as an open dataset. A companion SOHAM HAMSO YouTube channel accompanies the reader.
